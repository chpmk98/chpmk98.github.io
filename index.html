Alvin Tan

About

Alvin designs technological solutions to ill-defined problems in the area of cyberphysical systems. His research includes social robot navigation, ubiquitous sensor networking, adaptive residential energy demand, and standby-power reduction for environmental and structural sensing.

His aspirational career goals are to bring order out of chaos and to cultivate human flourishing, and he has been actively progressing towards this by building his technical competency and contributing to projects that matter. His technical skillset covers coding, computing architecture, and logical reasoning, which he developed during his undergrad through coursework and class projects. During his Ph.D., he grew in his ability to deeply self-study a subject area, distill key concepts, identify gaps in the state-of-practice, and triage between several potential courses of action to best distribute time and effort towards useful project output.

He leveraged these technical skills in several meaningful projects -- spanning uncertainty-aware social robot navigation, privacy-preserving sensor networks, deep neural networks for climate forecasting, and residential DC microgrids for household panel capacity management -- where he worked with front-line researchers in UC Berkeley, Lawrence Berkeley National Lab, and Johns Hopkins University Applied Physics Lab. These projects have yielded both academic artifacts in publications and presentations, as well as several open-source repositories for other researchers and developers to continue building off of, putting him well on his way to achieving his career goals.

Currently, Alvin is an EECS Ph.D. candidate at UC Berkeley and will graduate in Dec 2026. He graduated summa cum laude with undergraduate honors from Northwestern University in 2020 with a B.S. in Computer Engineering, Economics and Mathematics.
