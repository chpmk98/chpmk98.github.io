ABOUT

I design systems for a world where technology keeps getting closer to us -- into our homes, on our streets, and in the decisions made around us. That closeness creates new ways of getting it wrong, like acting unsafely around people, leaking what it senses about them, and overselling what it can actually do. My work closes that gap: building systems that know their own limits, and stress-testing the ones that don't.

During my PhD, I added uncertainty estimation to a reinforcement-learning robot navigator to recognize unfamiliar pedestrians and give them a wider berth, then deployed it on a TurtleBot2 running ROS2. Other projects include an opportunistic sensor network that routes data through passing smartphones without exposing their owners' whereabouts; a study stress-testing a state-of-the-art foundation model for climate forecasting to see where it holds up and where it doesn't; and residential energy systems that keep a home's increasing electrical demand within what its panel can handle. I did this work with researchers at UC Berkeley, Lawrence Berkeley National Lab, and Johns Hopkins University's Applied Physics Laboratory, and it produced several papers and open-source repositories.

I am an EECS PhD candidate at UC Berkeley, graduating in December 2026. Before Berkeley, I earned a BS from Northwestern in 2020, graduating summa cum laude with honors and a triple major in computer engineering, economics, and mathematics.
